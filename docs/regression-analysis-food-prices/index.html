BA240, Academic Project

Regression Analysis, Food Prices and Taste Scores

A simple linear regression on 168 New York City restaurants examining whether food taste score predicts price per person per meal.

Carolina Varanko, BA240, Spring 2025

01. The Question

What is being tested

Does a restaurant's food taste score predict its price per person per meal in New York City?

This project tests that relationship using simple linear regression. Food taste score is the independent variable. Price per person per meal is the dependent variable. The goal is to measure how strongly, and in what direction, taste relates to price across a sample of New York City restaurants.

03. Distribution Shape

How each variable is spread

Food Taste Score

Left skewed. Most scores fall between 19 and 23. The distribution roughly follows the empirical rule, with about 68% of scores within 1 standard deviation of the mean, about 95% within 2 standard deviations, and nearly 100% within 3 standard deviations.

No outliers detected.

Price Per Person

Right skewed. Most restaurants charge between $38 and $52. The distribution does not fully satisfy the empirical rule, with fewer observations within 1 standard deviation than a normal distribution would predict.

The 25th percentile is $36.00 and the 75th percentile is $50.00, giving a standard 1.5 times IQR bound of $15.00 to $71.00. No outliers detected. The minimum price of $19.00 and the maximum price of $65.00 both fall within that range.

Note: this differs from the original PDF, which listed a $22.50 bound and 10 outliers. Recalculating the 25th percentile directly from the full 168 row dataset gives $36.00, not $39.00, which changes the bound and outlier count shown above.

04. The Regression

Equation and fit

Y = 2.94X − 17.83 Y = predicted price per person, X = food taste score
Multiple R0.627
R Square0.393
Adjusted R Square0.390
Standard Error7.26
Observations168

R squared of 0.393 means about 39.3% of the variation in price is explained by taste score. About 60.7% is explained by other factors. Multiple R of 0.627 indicates a moderate positive correlation.

What does R squared mean in plain terms?

Price per person is shaped by many things. Taste score accounts for about 39% of the differences in price across restaurants.

The other 61% comes from factors not measured here, such as location, restaurant type, or service quality.

07. Assumption Checks

Regression assumptions

Assumption Status Explanation
Mean of residuals equals 0 Satisfied Residuals average near 0
Linearity Satisfied Scatterplot and trendline show a linear pattern
Constant variance Satisfied Residuals appear evenly spread across fitted values
Independence Satisfied Each observation is a different restaurant
Normality of residuals Partially met Price is right skewed, which carries into the residuals. Large sample size helps.
What do these assumptions mean?

Mean of residuals equals 0. On average, the model's price guesses are not too high or too low.

Linearity. A straight line reasonably describes how taste score relates to price.

Constant variance. The model's errors stay about the same size across all taste scores.

Independence. One restaurant's price does not affect another restaurant's price.

Normality of residuals. The model's errors mostly follow a bell shaped pattern, which helps the statistics behave predictably.

08. Summary

Takeaway

Better tasting food is associated with higher menu prices in New York City. Taste score alone explains less than half the variation in price. Other factors such as location, restaurant type, and service quality likely account for the remainder.
